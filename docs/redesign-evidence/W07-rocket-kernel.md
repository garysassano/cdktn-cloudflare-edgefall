# Rocket flight and impact kernel

Released rockets now have deterministic acceleration, limited target steering, swept contact and a single explosion damage budget. The implementation is platform-neutral and uses accepted ticks, Q256 positions and authored integer heading vectors. Launcher input, ammunition, room snapshots/checkpoints and the playable weapon laboratory are the next integration step; these released-projectile fixtures do not establish that the launcher is available in a room.

The [launcher definitions](../../src/game/content/weapons/rocket-launcher.ts) prepare the proposed 24-tick cadence and 20-round pickup for registration. Flight starts at two logical pixels per tick, accelerates by half a pixel per tick to six, and expires after 90 motion ticks. The 4×4 flight body and 48-pixel blast radius are separate shapes. A content guard requires the blast to enclose the flight body, so a contacted target cannot accidentally fall outside the explosion.

## Guidance and contact

There are 32 authored Q14 heading vectors, clockwise from right. Guidance can change one heading step every three ticks and stays within four steps of the launch direction. It acquires within two heading steps and 240 pixels, and retains a lock within four steps and 256 pixels. These cones use quantized headings. A visible existing target keeps its lock even if a nearer enemy appears; lost, covered or ineligible targets are dropped immediately, with reacquisition every six ticks. Target centers are sampled at the beginning of the motion tick. Neutral solids, allies and the owner are ineligible guidance targets; collision and blast material rules still apply independently.

Eligible candidates are ordered by squared distance, entity ID and collider ID. Concrete, shield faces and damageable solids occlude guidance. Flight uses the same exact swept query as bullets, including relative target/terrain motion and terrain-before-shield-before-body ties. No runtime trigonometry, random spread, renderer callback or wall-clock time enters the kernel.

The first contact selects the explosion origin and rational tick fraction. Moving hurtboxes and cover are sampled at that fraction, which prevents a blast from damaging a victim that arrives later or missing one that leaves later. The contact itself grants zero additional direct damage. A body receives at most one four-damage blast hit; overlapping hurtboxes do not multiply it. Shield occlusion retains the existing material response. The contacted entity takes one of the sixteen available blast slots when eligible, with the remaining slots ordered deterministically. Expiry produces no phantom explosion, while contact on the final motion tick still resolves.

The crowd regression first reproduced an omitted direct target: sixteen lower-ID neighbors consumed the blast cap before the struck enemy. Reserving that enemy within the same cap fixes the omission without increasing the cap or adding a second damage grant. Separate moving-victim and moving-wall controls distinguish the contact-time geometry from the end-of-tick geometry.

## Validation and scope

`pnpm check` passes 715 tests across 70 files, including twenty-one rocket cases and the existing collision, combat, asset, audio, type, bundle and infrastructure checks. Node 24.20.0, Chromium 151.0.7922.34 and local workerd through Wrangler 4.128.0 agree on all fourteen trajectories: four guided firing directions, lifetime expiry, thin/moving cover, crossing and moving victims, shields, overlapping hurtboxes, crowded impact, and covered/dead lock replacement. The fixture covers 263 flight transitions and 56 serialized continuation points, including fourteen births and 42 states after motion. Candidate-order reversal preserves every result.

The retained [evidence package](rocket-kernel-2026-09-08/artifacts.json) contains six artifacts totaling 225,019 bytes and 224 source fingerprints. It includes the full conformance report, compact rocket results, exact browser bundle, project check and the earlier failing clock/body-bounds controls. Its manifest SHA-256 is `6a53b8eb96cf1597c22a47cb57c658edf710b59c0005829150cfb6073638703a`. The rebuilt browser bundle and composite runtime-input hash match the final conformance run. The report's commit identifies the preceding base; its source and bundle hashes identify the tested increment.

Private rocket state retains the launch/current heading, speed, lock identity and acquisition/turn clocks. Duplicate or skipped motion steps, impossible age/speed combinations, out-of-leash headings, regressed or phase-shifted guidance clocks, invalid geometry and ambiguous collider IDs are rejected. The shared projectile query validates the full body at both movement endpoints even with no collision candidates. Serialized continuation compares every remaining step at each selected boundary. This is JSON state-continuation evidence; durable room archive validation and SQLite recovery for rockets remain part of the integration step.

The current W06 art, runtime media and published review artifacts are unchanged. Launcher art/audio still follow the pending style review. The next increment must register the launcher in the combat catalog, debit one round per accepted fire action, preserve held/onset deduplication and sidearm fallback, expose diagnostic flight/blast state, and retain guidance through room recovery. Full weapon coverage, production v3, host/deployed timing, live trace ingestion and the Harbor/Foundry/Carrier campaign remain open. Conformance suite duration is not a per-tick CPU profile.
