# W05 grenades on moving platforms

The deterministic combat laboratory now has an `ordnance` scenario with a moving lift and a closing press. Grenades inherit player/platform velocity at their hand release, ride settled supports, rebound relative to moving surfaces and resolve a proved crush without aborting the room. This completes the previously unspecified grenade platform policies; the [W05 acceptance audit](W05-acceptance-audit.md) identifies the remaining HMG and integrated terrain-boundary work.

## Policies and authority

The release uses the accepted end-of-tick player pose and end-of-tick terrain clearance. A standing throw adds a facing-relative 2.5-pixel horizontal and -4.5-pixel vertical impulse; a crouched throw uses 4 and -1.5. The throw inherits the player's own velocity plus its currently accepted support velocity once, clamping that inherited term to eight pixels per axis. On the one-pixel-per-tick lift, neutral standing launch is 3.5 pixels/tick, running right is 6.5 and running left is -4.5. An airborne throw inherits body velocity with no former support contribution. The accepted grenade velocity bounds are twelve horizontal, twelve and a half upward and eight downward pixels per tick.

Airborne grenade velocity is world velocity. Settled grenade velocity excludes carry, so the shared solver adds its support displacement once. A rebound reflects half the incoming velocity relative to the struck surface, applies three-quarter tangential speed at a vertical contact, clamps to the declared bounds and detaches. The solver can suppress support carry for that airborne interval; this prevents a descending lift from adding its velocity a second time while a rebound separates from it. Support removal produces ordinary falling without restarting the fuse. The existing maximum of three bounces remains.

A proved vertical or horizontal crush destroys the grenade, consumes no additional stock, refunds none and produces one zero-damage terrain impact. It does not explode. The terminal contact position is never accepted as a surviving grenade body. Crush takes precedence if it coincides with the fuse boundary. Initial overlap or another unresolved collision remains a transaction failure; numerical uncertainty cannot manufacture a crush. An uncrushed grenade still detonates after ninety flight ticks, using the existing radius/material/occlusion damage policy.

The lift moves right one pixel per tick through tick 50. The press descends two pixels per tick from tick 56 through 85. Both trajectories derive from the authoritative world tick, project their exact shape/position/motion through the existing platform records and reconstruct during journal replay. The new profile and trajectories enter the content digest. Protocol 3.7, archive 8 and recording format 5 retain their existing shapes; body support, velocity and bounce count already contain the required private grenade continuation.

The new scene also exposed an existing encounter progress record that serialized the full body: two simultaneous contacts made it 313 characters at ticks 76–77, above the 256-character observation bound. Progress now records only root position, own velocity, support and facing. The observation bound and watchdog rules are unchanged.

Focused cases cover actual standing/running/crouched releases, airborne and lift momentum bounds, lift reversal, relative rebound, descending-lift separation, removed support, vertical/horizontal crush at the fuse tick, insertion-order independence, unresolved-overlap rejection and exact private recovery. Existing weapon, shield, tank and campaign tests pass.

Node, Chromium and local workerd agree on two 120-tick four-input runs, each including 480 duplicate packets and forty movement reconciliations. The early run releases at tick 5 and checks nine checkpoint/journal and binary snapshot boundaries at ticks 1, 4, 5, 49, 50, 55, 82, 83 and 95. The delayed run reproduces the browser’s later throw, beginning at tick 6 and releasing at tick 10, with boundaries at 6, 9, 10, 50, 76, 81, 82, 83 and 100. Both runs crush all four grenades at 83. Each player retains nine grenades and three lives; enemies take no damage. The early final state/trace hashes are `21c973ac` / `90708c74`; delayed hashes are `241f4db9` / `a3aa11ea`. Snapshots range from 1,768 to 1,960 bytes. These are small correctness fixtures, not load or tick CPU measurements.

The SQLite Durable Object verifier restores both scenarios in fresh workerd processes at those eighteen boundaries and each final tick 120: twenty cold-process boundaries in total. Every saved prefix matches its cold reconstruction exactly, including platforms, active grenades, event ownership and stock/lives. Each journal segment injects transaction failure, verifies the unchanged prefix, then commits and restores. Both scenarios remain inside the six-row archive bound and finish with the same final hashes as the portable runs. Existing tank, area, shield, foot-combat, rifle, life, campaign, loading and phase recovery checks also pass.

The four-keyboard browser run releases all throws at tick 10, consumes all four crush impacts at 83 and reaches snapshot 129 in every client. Fifteen later snapshots and twelve event receipts agree; the injected duplicate reader sees twenty duplicates and consumes each effect once. All players retain three lives and nine grenades. Its later throw exposed a solver defect: integer rounding left a witnessed one-subpixel gap, but the retained plane constraint acted as though the gap were already closed. The correction preserves the one-subpixel witness limit, four-contact budget and failure handling. A direct regression reproduces the captured body, and a separate sweep of sixty throw-start timings completes without a world failure. The failed browser, diagnostics and minimal movement diagnosis are retained.

Final browser diagnostics contain only favicon 404s and canceled loopback admission/session/profile requests; no page exception, protocol, persistence or room clock failure is present. There are 191 independent host-clock samples with no backward wall-clock step. This short observation does not close the earlier host-clock gate.
