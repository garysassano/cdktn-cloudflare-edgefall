# Diagnostic combat archive format 13

The combat laboratory uses archive **13**, simulation/recording format **10**, protocol **3.11** and combat snapshot section **3**. This extends [format 12](combat-checkpoint-v12.md) with released rocket continuation. Older archive and recording formats are rejected; there is no migration that invents missing guidance state. Production v3 integration remains open.

The private `rockets` array holds at most 32 rockets within the shared 256-attack budget. Every rocket retains its entity, owner, team and action identities; attack definition; position and velocity; birth and current ticks; launch/current heading and speed; target identity; and acquisition/turn deadlines. The checkpoint validator requires exact fields, a known player owner, a unique attack entity and one rocket per fire action. A lock may name an enemy removed later in the same accepted tick; the following guidance step drops it. Unknown lock identities are rejected.

Every retained rocket must be at the checkpoint tick with age below its 90-tick lifetime. Speed must match age and the authored acceleration cap, velocity must match its integer heading, and the heading must stay within the launch leash. Acquisition and turn clocks must be in the future, within their respective intervals and at their original birth-relative phase. Full flight-body bounds are checked even without collision candidates. Missing clocks, stale ticks, altered motion and duplicate charges fail before encoding or restoring state.

The existing 48-byte projectile wire record carries rocket flight using attack ID 17, body shape 19, the current 0–31 heading, velocity, position, birth tick and lifetime. Shape 20 describes the distinct blast footprint; shapes 17 and 18 remain the existing moving platforms. Private target and guidance clocks stay in the archive. The content identity includes the launcher profile and authored content, so incompatible guidance policies cannot resume a room merely because their wire layout matches.

Explosion notices use the existing acknowledged event contract and retain source entity/action/owner, birth tick and blast position. The source must have been released on an earlier tick within the lifetime. Flight contact selects the rational geometry time for the single blast damage budget; it does not add a second direct hit. Quiet expiry emits no explosion. A blocked muzzle still spends the accepted charge and produces no missile or blast. Released rockets survive owner death; a campaign continue clears them alongside other outgoing attacks.

The [launcher integration evidence](redesign-evidence/W07-rocket-launcher.md) separates portable archive replay, actual SQLite restarts, the keyboard inspector and the four-browser authoritative room from final media and deployed acceptance.
