# Diagnostic combat archive format 15

Historical format 15 contract. Current material-aware continuation uses [format 16](combat-checkpoint-v16.md); format 15 archives are rejected.

The combat laboratory uses archive **15**, simulation/recording format **12**, protocol **3.13** and combat snapshot section **4**. This extends [format 14](combat-checkpoint-v14.md) with individual weapon supplies, exact contact-entry continuation and confirmed pickup events. Earlier diagnostic archives and recordings reject explicitly. Breakwater mission/recording format **3** also rejects its previous embedded combat layout. Production v3 integration remains open.

Private `pickups` state retains format 1, the committed tick, ordered item status/resolution/claimant records and contact-entry latches. Its tick must equal the world tick. At most 64 items and 256 contacts are permitted. The content identity binds definitions for each party size, including item and claim IDs, source, weapon, ammunition/capacity, activation/expiry and stationary support geometry. Item IDs cannot collide with any other gameplay entity, and the entity allocator must remain above every item ID. Terminal availability cannot regress within a run.

Pickup evaluation runs after accepted movement and damage, before publication. Contact order follows the collision solver's residual intervals, including stops and slides, then player slot and claim ID. A matching weapon adds capped ammunition; a different gun replaces it while preserving cooldown, shot ordinal, last action ID and control epoch. Replacing a gun cancels its remaining firearm markers and attached beam/flame ownership while retaining released attacks and independent knife/grenade actions. Ineligible, fully supplied or already overlapping actors cannot consume another item. Destroying support retires the item at that accepted boundary.

Each `pickupClaims` record preserves item/claim/source identity, owner and slot, tick, previous weapon/ammunition and final weapon/ammunition. Validation binds claims to that tick's resolved item, requires one claim per item, checks successive grants and the owner's final inventory, and rejects contact latches outside the saved body geometry. Retained event history cannot contain a second claim for the same item. The [public snapshot](snapshot-v3.md) carries item availability and attribution; private contacts stay in the archive. The [acknowledged event](events-v3.md) carries the confirmed inventory grant, and rendering or replaying it cannot grant ammunition.

The world, inventory, pickup state, event cursor and all controlling acknowledgments form one candidate transaction. A failed candidate leaves the previous state and input queues intact. SQLite commits preserve that same boundary with checkpoint compaction and journal-prefix validation. Loading re-simulates the committed journal, including contact latches, and compares its asserted state hash. A storage restart does not recreate a consumed item or repeat a grant.

Ordinary connection replacement, empty-room pause and recovery preserve item resolution and latches. Recovery starts a fresh transport/event generation, restores current availability and inventory, and does not replay its old claim effects. A paid campaign continue restarts the authored checkpoint supplies under the new run epoch, resets contacts and current claim notices, and restores baseline player equipment. Supplies whose absolute diagnostic expiry has passed remain expired. Static content and claim IDs are scoped by that run epoch; transport recovery alone never resets them.

The `pickups` diagnostic route supplies four unreserved items for each finite weapon family and one rear item that expires at tick 24. The `support` route places a shotgun supply on the damageable scaffold. These engineering routes exercise availability, sharing, accepted swaps and permanent support loss without requiring final pickup art. The full campaign, final media and deployed cadence/telemetry remain separate gates.
