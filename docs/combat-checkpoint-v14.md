# Diagnostic combat archive format 14

The combat laboratory now uses archive **14**, simulation/recording format **11**, protocol **3.12** and combat snapshot section **3**. This extends [format 13](combat-checkpoint-v13.md) with attached laser charges and acknowledged beam geometry. Older archive, recording and protocol versions are rejected; no continuation state is invented for an older experiment. Production v3 integration remains open.

The private `beams` array contains at most four charges within the shared 256-attack budget. Each charge retains its entity, owner, team and action identities; attack definition; birth and current ticks; muzzle origin; cardinal heading; and clipped length. Exact fields are required, each owner may retain only one beam, and the action must still be that living unseated player's laser action. The birth tick must match the action start, the beam tick must equal the checkpoint tick, and its origin and heading must match the accepted muzzle. Length and age must fit the negotiated profile. The profile is included in the combat content identity.

A charge born at tick 1 damages only at that tick, follows the muzzle through tick 6 while held, and expires before the next accepted charge at tick 7. Its continuation state contains no pending damage grant. Release, owner death, action interruption or a muzzle blocked by movement cancels the attached beam. Pause, waiting-connection replacement and run recovery settle affected beams while preserving paid energy and cooldown. A campaign continue clears them with other outgoing attacks. A storage restart at an accepted boundary restores the paid state exactly; replaying the journal then advances consecutive ticks without repeating the birth pulse.

Public snapshots project the stored clipped length as contiguous 48-byte area-exposure records. An unobstructed 512-pixel beam uses two 256-pixel segments, with one attack/action identity and consecutive lobe indexes. The public lifetime is the six-tick paid interval; `attached` is true. Stored geometry is preserved at the committed boundary even if that pulse killed a target. The next accepted step recalculates clipping from current geometry without granting damage.

Every successful laser shot notice and its acknowledged [gameplay event](events-v3.md) retain the birth muzzle, clipped length, width and heading alongside the existing player/control-epoch/shot-ordinal confirmation. The exact event record is now 76 bytes. Geometry is required only for beam shot definitions and must match their registered profile; other events carry zero beam words. A blocked muzzle still spends energy and produces a `muzzle-blocked` event with no beam. Snapshot loss therefore cannot erase a short pulse while its acknowledged event remains in the retention window. Displaying a received pulse never repeats damage or an energy debit.

The [laser integration evidence](redesign-evidence/W07-laser-integration.md) distinguishes accepted input and formal archive replay, actual SQLite process restarts, the keyboard inspector and four-browser room delivery from final media and deployed acceptance.
