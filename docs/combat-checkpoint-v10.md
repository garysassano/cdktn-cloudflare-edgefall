# Diagnostic combat archive format 10

The combat laboratory uses archive **10**, protocol **3.9**, combat snapshot section **3**, and simulation/recording format **7**. Earlier experimental schemas are rejected. This extends the retained [HMG continuation contract](combat-checkpoint-v9.md) with destructible world geometry; it does not migrate the deployed v2 product.

Each world retains an ordered `props` array. A prop owns its authored entity/definition identity, remaining health, destruction tick, destroying actor and destroying action. Definitions supply immutable collision rectangles and maximum health. The checkpoint validates the complete authored roster, damage bounds and destruction ownership, including actors that have since left the visible enemy section. The content digest includes the scaffold definition.

All attacks in a tick query the same starting geometry. Damageable solids compete with concrete and hurtboxes in earliest-hit queries and occlude attacks behind their faces. Ordered accepted impacts reduce health; the first lethal impact records destruction once. The committed boundary removes the solid, advances geometry revision and clears lost support/contacts without changing body position or velocity. Normal gravity advances the following tick. Enemy falls resolve through the existing terminal physical boundary and encounter ledger; a prop's destroyer is not automatically credited with an enemy kill.

Full snapshots carry the same prop records, removals and geometry revision. A combat section has a 52-byte header and at most 32 additional 24-byte prop records. The maximum complete frame allocation is 52,044 bytes; this is a decoder bound rather than a measured traffic rate. Clients validate loaded definitions and irreversible removal history before installing changed geometry and replaying retained commands. Unknown geometry still fails closed.

Checkpoint/journal validation couples private props, public props, contact state, geometry revision and encounter attribution at the same accepted boundary. An invalid candidate or failed SQLite transaction cannot publish damage or remove support. Cold recovery retains destruction; an explicit checkpoint continue starts the authored geometry again in its new run epoch. The [support evidence](redesign-evidence/W05-support.md) records the validation scope and outstanding gates.
